Story, scope, and evidence. A simple checklist for showcasing work.

## The Portfolio Paradox

Everyone says you need a portfolio. Few explain what makes one good. The result: galleries of screenshots that show output without context.

Screenshots show what. Portfolios should show how and why.

## The Three Elements

### Story

Every project has a narrative:
- What problem existed?
- What constraints shaped the solution?
- What decisions did you make and why?
- What was the outcome?

Without story, work is decoration.

### Scope

Be clear about your role:
- What did YOU do?
- Who else was involved?
- What was your specific contribution?

Claiming credit for team work is transparent. Understating your contribution is equally harmful.

### Evidence

Show, don't just tell:
- Before/after comparisons
- Metrics that moved
- User feedback
- Process artifacts

Claims without evidence are just claims.

## Curation Over Volume

Three excellent case studies beat twenty screenshots.

Select projects that:
- Show range across skills
- Demonstrate your best work
- Tell interesting stories
- Match the roles you want

Remove anything that doesn't elevate the whole.

## The Unseen Work

Some of your best work might be:
- Systems you built
- Problems you prevented
- Processes you improved
- Knowledge you spread

Find ways to make invisible work visible.

## Keep It Current

A stale portfolio suggests you've stopped growing. Even if projects are old, update:
- Your bio
- Your focus areas
- Your contact info
- The design itself

The portfolio itself is a project. Treat it like one.
