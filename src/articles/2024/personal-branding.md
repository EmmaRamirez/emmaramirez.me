How to show your work online without feeling like a billboard.

## The Cringe Factor

"Personal branding" triggers an involuntary eye-roll for many of us. It conjures images of motivational LinkedIn posts, carefully curated highlight reels, and performative authenticity.

But sharing your work doesn't have to feel gross.

## Work in Public, Not Performance

The difference is intent. Performance asks "how will this make me look?" Working in public asks "what am I learning that might help others?"

When you write about a problem you actually struggled with, the authenticity shows. When you manufacture content for engagement, that shows too.

## Small Arenas

You don't need a massive following. You need to be known by the right people—the ones who might hire you, collaborate with you, or learn from you.

That might be:
- A well-maintained GitHub profile
- Occasional blog posts about your craft
- Thoughtful contributions in niche communities
- Speaking at local meetups

## The Portfolio Paradox

The best portfolios aren't comprehensive—they're curated. Three excellent case studies beat twenty screenshots. Show your process, not just your output.

> Quality of attention beats quantity of eyeballs.

## Sustainable Practice

Post when you have something to say, not on a schedule designed for algorithms. Long gaps are fine. Forced content is worse than silence.

The goal isn't content creation. It's leaving useful artifacts of your journey that others can find.

## Your Domain, Your Rules

Own your space online. A personal site that you control beats any platform. Platforms change. Your domain stays.

Keep it simple. Keep it honest. Let your work speak.
