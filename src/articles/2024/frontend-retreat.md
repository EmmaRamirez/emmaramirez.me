Three days without Wi-Fi forced me to diagram every component by hand. It was surprisingly clarifying.

## Forced Disconnection

The retreat wasn't supposed to be offline. But when the cabin's internet died on day one, we had a choice: leave or adapt. We adapted.

## Drawing Components

Without the ability to prototype in code, we sketched. Boxes for components. Arrows for data flow. Handwritten notes in the margins about edge cases.

Something shifted. Without the temptation to jump into implementation, we actually designed. We questioned assumptions. We found problems before writing a single line of code.

## The Paper Prototype

By day two, we had covered an entire wall with diagrams. The architecture emerged visually before it emerged technically. Consensus formed around shared drawings rather than competing codebases.

> Sometimes the best way to think about code is to not write any.

## What We Brought Back

When we returned to our keyboards, implementation went faster than expected. The hard decisions were already made. The component boundaries were already drawn. We were executing a plan rather than discovering one.

## A New Practice

Now I start complex features with paper. Not always for three days, but always for at least an hour. The habit stuck because it works.
