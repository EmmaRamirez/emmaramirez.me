An early-morning ritual for testing palettes against real content.

## The Morning Test

Before the day's work begins, coffee in hand, I test yesterday's color decisions against real content. Not lorem ipsum. Not design-tool previews. Real words on a real screen.

It's remarkable how often things look different at 7am than they did at 11pm.

## Why Mornings

Fresh eyes catch what tired eyes miss. That hover state that seemed subtle is actually invisible. The accent color that felt bold now reads as aggressive.

Mornings reset the visual palette.

## The Setup

1. **Real content** — Actual articles, actual data
2. **Multiple devices** — Phone, laptop, external monitor
3. **Both modes** — Light and dark, side by side
4. **Ambient light** — Natural light if possible

The goal isn't formal testing. It's living with the design for a few minutes before committing.

## What I Catch

**Contrast failures** that passed tools but fail eyes. WCAG compliance is a floor, not a ceiling. If it feels hard to read, it is hard to read.

**Hierarchy confusion** where too many elements compete. Morning clarity reveals the noise.

**Color meanings** that shifted. The warning yellow that felt urgent now feels sickly.

## A Simple Ritual

Coffee. Content. Colors. Fifteen minutes of actual looking before the meetings start.

Most days, nothing changes. But the days when something does? Those are the days that matter.
