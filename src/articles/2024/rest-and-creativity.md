Why stepping away from the screen often solves the layout faster.

## The Grind Mythology

Tech culture celebrates the all-nighter. The heroic push through. Sleeping under desks. As if exhaustion equals commitment.

But creative work doesn't follow factory rules.

## The Incubation Effect

Cognitive science has a name for what happens when you step away: incubation. Your subconscious continues working on the problem while your conscious mind rests.

That layout that wouldn't come together? Walk away. Make dinner. Sleep on it. The solution often arrives unbidden.

## Diminishing Returns

After a certain point—research suggests around 4-6 hours of focused creative work—quality degrades faster than quantity increases.

More hours ≠ more output. Often it equals worse output that you'll have to redo tomorrow.

## Deliberate Rest

Rest isn't passive. It can be:

- **Walking** — Movement unlocks thought
- **Sleeping** — Memory consolidation and insight
- **Different work** — Switching contexts refreshes capacity
- **Nature** — Attention restoration is real

## The Permission Problem

Many of us feel guilty resting. We've internalized that productivity equals worth.

But rest is productive. It's part of the work, not a break from it.

> You can't pour from an empty cup. You also can't design from an empty mind.

## A Practice

End work at the hard part. Leave a problem unsolved before stepping away. Let your subconscious have something to work on overnight.

Then trust the process. The solution will come.
