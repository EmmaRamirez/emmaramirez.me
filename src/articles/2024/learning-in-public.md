Shipping drafts, sharing mistakes, and the upside of leaving footprints.

## The Expert Trap

We wait until we're "qualified" to share. Until we've mastered the topic. Until our thoughts are polished and defensible.

We wait forever.

## Drafts as Documentation

Learning in public means sharing before you're ready. Writing about the thing you're struggling with right now, not the thing you mastered last year.

The value isn't authority—it's documentation. Your struggle today is someone else's search query tomorrow.

## Mistakes as Markers

When you share your failures, two things happen:

1. Others feel less alone in their own failures
2. Your future self has a record of what didn't work

Hide your mistakes and you're doomed to repeat them. Share them and they become useful.

## The Footprint Effect

Every blog post, every tweet, every public commit creates a trail. Over time, these footprints compound:

- They demonstrate growth
- They build an archive of your thinking
- They attract like-minded people
- They create serendipitous opportunities

## Starting Small

You don't need a blog with a posting schedule. You can:

- Keep a public learning journal
- Tweet your "til" (today I learned)
- Document solutions in GitHub issues
- Answer questions on Stack Overflow

The medium matters less than the practice.

> The best time to start was years ago. The second best time is now.

## The Long Game

Learning in public isn't about building an audience. It's about building a habit of reflection and sharing that makes you a better learner.

The audience, if it comes, is a side effect.
